## 2. The single question that changed

> **"Did Cornell Police adequately investigate the incident? Why didn't they bring in the
> Ithaca Police?"**

Every other answer on the page was left untouched.

### Version A — original (as first published)

> "The Cornell University Police Department investigated the matter in 2024 and **shared all
> investigatory information** with the Tompkins County District Attorney's Office, which is the
> established protocol if the department believes it is possible a crime has occurred. The Cornell
> University Police Department is a **sworn law enforcement agency and performs the same
> functions as municipal police**, but on Cornell property. This incident occurred in Cornell's
> jurisdiction."

### Version B — edit 1, Wednesday Sep 30 afternoon

The entire disclosure sentence was **deleted**, not corrected — leaving an answer that no longer
addressed the question asked. The police description was also changed:

> "The Cornell University Police Department is a unit of the university, and its officers are
> **sworn Tompkins County deputy sheriffs** whose duties include the enforcement of law and order
> on Cornell property. This incident occurred in Cornell's jurisdiction."

### Version C — edit 2, Thursday Oct 1 (current live text)

The disclosure claim returned, **narrowed** from "all investigatory information" to two named
items. The police description changed a second time:

> "The Cornell University Police Department investigated the matter in 2024 and shared **the
> victim's sworn statement and the Snapchat screenshot** with the Tompkins County District
> Attorney's Office, which is the established protocol if the department believes it is possible a
> crime has occurred. The Cornell University Police Department is a unit of the university, and its
> officers are **special deputy sheriffs pursuant to NY Educational Law 5709** whose duties include
> the enforcement of law and order on Cornell property. This incident occurred in Cornell's
> jurisdiction."

## 3. The legal-status drift in the police description

The CUPD characterisation was rewritten in **both** edits, and the final rewrite altered the
legal category being asserted:

| Version | Assertion |
|---|---|
| A | "sworn law enforcement agency … performs the same functions as municipal police" |
| B | "a unit of the university … **sworn** Tompkins County deputy sheriffs" |
| C | "a unit of the university … **special** deputy sheriffs **pursuant to NY Educational Law 5709**" |

Both edits moved the description *away from* the "same functions as municipal police" framing and
toward "a unit of the university." The B→C step swapped "sworn" for "special" — different legal
categories — and added a statutory citation.

## 4. The contradiction that was never corrected

A **separate** answer on the same page was never edited in any version:

> **"Did Cornell provide the Snapchat screenshot to the District Attorney?"**
> "The Cornell University Police Department **provided it as part of the evidence supplied to the
> District Attorney in November 2024**."

This flat assertion contradicts the on-record position of Tompkins County District Attorney
Matthew Van Houten:

- **ABC News (Sep 28):** his office "never received this Snapchat image."
- **CNN / Jake Tapper:** "We did not see them initially. They were not part of our initial
  analysis. But I understand that Cornell had them and they sent them to our team."
- **WSKG (Sep 30):** "If it's [the transcript] factual, we absolutely should have been informed
  about what Jane Doe said to police that was not in her 6 page statement."

Cornell revised the *general* disclosure sentence twice but left the *specific* November 2024
Snapchat-transmission claim standing in the live page as of 2026-10-03. The two answers in the
current FAQ are not reconcilable with each other without further clarification.

## 4a. Related omission reported by the New York Times

NYT reporting (surfaced via PBS NewsHour, Oct 1, and the Chronicle liveblog) states that the
statement CUPD supplied to the DA was a **condensed six-page summary of an hours-long interview**
which omitted Doe's strongest language, including:

> "I can say with 100 percent confidence I was raped."

NYT also reported, per the Chronicle liveblog, that CUPD "had conducted additional interviews and
alerted the district attorney's office to that fact." Counsel for Jane Doe, **Thomas Giuffra**,
separately confirmed the "100 percent confidence" language to NPR. Neither WSKG nor NPR obtained
the interview transcript itself.
