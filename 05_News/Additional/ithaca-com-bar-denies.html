Jane Doe Lawsuit Accuses 7 Cornell Fraternity Members of Gang Rape; Bar Denies Serving Underage Student

A former Cornell University student has filed a civil lawsuit against seven fraternity members, the university, the Chi Phi fraternity and a local bar over an alleged gang rape in 2024, prompting Tompkins County prosecutors to reopen an investigation into the case.

A civil complaint demanding a jury trial was filed Sept. 16 in New York State Supreme Court in New York County by attorneys from Rheingold Giuffra Ruffo Plotkin & Hellman LLP alongside Cochrun & Seals, LLC.

According to the lawsuit, the then-20-year-old victim — referred to as Jane Doe — alleges seven members of the Xi Chapter of Chi Phi Fraternity sexually assaulted and gang raped her overnight between Oct. 19 and Oct. 20, 2024, at the fraternity house on Cornell University's Ithaca campus.

The lawsuit says the Xi Chapter was placed on temporary suspension on or about Nov. 8, 2024, for alleged Student Code of Conduct violations. The lawsuit says the seven student defendants were issued temporary suspensions around that time and “afforded the opportunity to mitigate their conduct by submitting essays to Cornell.”

In a Sept. 28 press release, Ithaca police said the incident was never reported to the department.

In the lawsuit, lawyers for Jane Doe said she was legally incapable of consenting to sexual activity due to severe intoxication. The lawsuit says the defendants pressured the victim into snorting ketamine at least three times during the incident.

The complaint also includes claims against Moonies Bar & Nightclub and its employees under New York’s Dram Shop Act for serving alcohol to a minor and visibly intoxicated individual.

The lawsuit says Jane Doe became intoxicated after drinking at Tri-Delta sorority's chapter house on Cornell's campus prior to taking an Uber to Moonies around 8:30 p.m. with the sorority's house mother. The lawsuit says Doe was marked with an "X" on each hand to indicate she was under 21, but she was still served at least one alcoholic beverage. Despite the presence of designated sorority monitors tasked with overseeing attendee safety and intoxication levels, the plaintiff and several sorority sisters left Moonies around 10 p.m. and walked to the nearby Lot 10 Bar and Lounge, where she received an under-21 wristband.

Moonies statement

In a Sept. 30 email to the Ithaca Times, a Moonies spokesperson said Doe’s claim that she was served alcohol by Moonies employees was “completely inaccurate.” The spokesperson said Moonies employees strictly adhered to state laws and event protocols during the Oct. 19, 2024, event for the Tri Delta sorority. The spokesperson said hand markings were used for the 35 guests under age 21 and wristbands for the 150 guests ages 21 and older.

“Our bartenders strongly followed the rules and only served guests who had wristbands on their hands,” the spokesperson said. “No alcohol was served to guests who had mark on their hands.”

The spokesperson said Moonies received no complaints in 2024 regarding intoxicated guests or serving alcohol to minors. The spokesperson said the business was never contacted by law enforcement or university police regarding the event or related issues.

The alleged assault

The lawsuit says Doe left Lot 10 around 11 p.m. and walked alone to the Chi Phi fraternity house at 107 Edgemoor Lane to meet one of the student defendants. The lawsuit says Doe consumed about 10 drinks over the previous three hours before her arrival. The defendant greeted her at the fraternity house and handed her a beer. The lawsuit omits if the beverage was unopened. The lawsuit says two of the defendants “plied (Doe) with marijuana and “Jamaican Liquor” believed to be some type of high-proof rum.” According to the lawsuit, the seven defendants gang raped the victim inside the fraternity house until around 5:45 a.m. the next day.

The lawsuit claims one defendant sent a message to a "Chi Phi Actives" Snapchat group chat, indicating Doe was available for sex in the fraternity house. The lawsuit includes a photograph of a phone screen displaying the group chat message.

“In doing so, (the defendant) organized and encouraged other Xi Chapter members to join in on the gang rape of (Doe) in the Xi Chapter fraternity house,” the lawsuit says.

The lawsuit says Doe’s friends encouraged her to report the incident on or about Nov. 8, 2024, to the Cornell University Police Department.

DA response

In a Sept. 28 press release, Tompkins County District Attorney Matthew Van Houten said recent community outrage prompted an explanation for why the 2024 investigation yielded no criminal charges, while also announcing the case’s reopening.

Van Houten said Cornell University Police Department asked his office to review Doe’s six-page, single-spaced typewritten statement, containing over 20 handwritten notations in November 2024. Van Houten said his office had to determine if her allegations constituted a crime under New York law, specifically regarding questions of legal consent.

“Jane Doe’s sworn statement in November of 2024 did not allege that she was drugged against her will or gang raped,” Van Houten said. “On the contrary, Jane Doe’s statement described her participation in drug use and sexual conduct as voluntary, conscious, and consensual.”

Van Houten said sexual assault offenses require proof beyond a reasonable doubt that the victim did not consent, stemming from forcible compulsion, mental incapacitation, physical helplessness or a victim's expressed refusal under New York state law. He said Doe’s statement failed to establish a lack of consent under any of those criteria.

Van Houten said prosecutors must strictly apply the law using only admissible evidence but noted that cases can be reopened if new evidence emerges as sexual assault victims take time to process their experiences. Van Houten said he contacted Doe’s attorneys after the lawsuit was filed to address discrepancies between her initial statement and the lawsuit's allegations.

“I have been asked by the community to revisit the decision whether to pursue criminal charges against the seven fraternity members,” Van Houten said. “I have already begun that process, starting with our conversation with Jane Doe and her attorneys.”

Van Houten said once appropriate charges are determined; the case will be presented to a Tompkins County grand jury. A senior female prosecutor with extensive experience handling sex crime trials, including cases involving college students, has been assigned to prepare the presentation. The grand jury will review Jane Doe’s testimony along with any new evidence raised in the civil lawsuit, and if the panel votes to indict, the DA's office will prosecute the charges.

Cornell statement

The Ithaca Times contacted Cornell University for comment on the lawsuit, and a spokesperson referred the publication to an online statement by Kyle Kimball, the vice president for university relations

Kimball said Cornell conducted a months-long Title IX investigation that included temporary suspensions. The investigation led to a multi-day hearing before a panel of faculty and staff that issued sanctions, including expulsions and suspensions. Kimball did not disclose the number of students expelled or suspended, nor did he specify the duration of any suspensions. Kimball said the Chi Phi fraternity chapter was closed in 2024 and remains barred from campus. His statement did not clarify whether the chapter was closed and barred as a direct result of the investigation or allegations.

In the statement, Kimball said, “none of the individuals charged were offered the opportunity to write essays as a sole consequence of their involvement.”

“Any suggestion that the university did not impose consequential punishments for those involved is false,” Kimball said. “A continuation of the false narrative that there were minimal consequences in this case is irresponsible, will make survivors feel less safe and supported, and will perpetuate the underreporting of sexual assault.”

Kimball said Cornell supports the DA’s decision to present the case to a criminal grand jury.

The case has sparked national attention and intense social media scrutiny over campus safety, the handling of sexual assault reporting and university disciplinary practices.

New York Gov. Kathy Hochul on Sept. 29called for an independent investigation into Cornell’s handling of the allegation.

“The allegations in this case are nothing short of horrifying,” Hochul said. “For any student to come forward after an experience like this takes extraordinary courage. No student should ever be left wondering whether the institutions meant to protect them will listen and act.”

Hochul supported Van Houten’s decision to reopen the case, but urged the hiring of an outside law firm to review the university’s reporting mechanisms, response procedures, campus culture and sexual violence prevention systems.

According to NBC News, Hochul announced on Oct. 1 that New York Attorney General Letitia James was appointed special prosecutor in the sexual assault investigation at Cornell. In a statement, the governor said newly released information raises serious questions that have undercut public faith in the probe. According to CBS News, Doe told campus police in a Nov. 14, 2024, interview, "I can say with 100% confidence I was raped." Campus police converted her transcript into a six-page summary for the district attorney's office, and Van Houten said his decision not to file charges was based on that summary without having seen the original transcript.
