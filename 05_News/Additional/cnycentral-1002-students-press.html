Students press Cornell on Chi Phi assault case; lawsuit names seven men, no charges yet

by De'Janee Ross

Thu, October 1, 2026 at 11:35 PM
(PHOTO BY CNY CENTRAL) (OCTOBER 1, 2026)

    topics:

  • Cornell
  • Chi Phi
  • Title IX
  • Sexual assault
  • Student Assembly
  • Lawsuit
  • Grand jury
  • Independent review

Cornell students are saying the university’s handling of a sexual assault case involving members of the Chi Phi fraternity has left many on campus questioning whether the school did enough to support survivors and hold students accountable.

The concerns came during a Student Assembly public hearing Thursday, where students were given an opportunity to speak about sexual violence, transparency and accountability at Cornell.

For some students, particularly those who are new to campus, the allegations have changed how they view the university.

“There’s been kind of like a lot of like just really bad energy that’s kind of been coming towards us,” said Alexandra Pogyo, a first-year student. “I think we haven’t really had a chance as students to express how we’re feeling about this situation, which is mainly we stand with Jane Doe we don’t think that’s being necessarily reflected by administration.”

Jane Doe is a former Cornell student who has alleged in a civil lawsuit that she was drugged and sexually assaulted by multiple members of Chi Phi in 2024. The lawsuit names seven men, none of whom have been criminally charged.

Cornell conducted its own Title IX process and has said it resulted in sanctions, including suspensions and expulsions. The university also closed the Chi Phi chapter, which remains barred from campus.

The Tompkins County district attorney has since reopened the criminal investigation and said the case is expected to be presented to a grand jury. Cornell has also agreed to an independent review of its handling of the case, while the New York attorney general’s office is conducting its own review.

Students at Thursday’s hearing said they want more than another discussion about the case. They called for greater transparency from Cornell and changes to how the university responds to sexual assault allegations.

Jane Wang, a sophomore who said she was speaking on behalf of Cornell’s American Civil Liberties Union chapter, said she believes Cornell’s response should have come sooner.

“Cornell’s response to the Jane Doe situation should have been something that happened from the get-go,” Wang said. “Two years ago when the initial filing of this case occurred, it should have been treated with this amount of urgency and this amount of respect.”

Wang said students need to feel that they can report incidents and be taken seriously.

She also said her criticism of the university comes from wanting to see Cornell improve.

“I don’t do this in an act to try to rebel against Cornell,” Wang said. “If anything, it’s because I value my place here at Cornell so much and because I am so invested in this school.”

Other students echoed calls for action and transparency.

“We want Cornell to make sure that they are actually supporting survivors and that they are supporting the student body, and that they are providing the necessary resources,” said Alexandra Pogyo.

“I would like to see more transparency in the punishments dealt out to students as well as more transparency in the process of how these punishments are deemed acceptable,” Colin Evans said.

Student leaders said they plan to continue pushing Cornell for greater transparency, including a proposed town hall with university administrators.

The hearing comes as the criminal investigation into the allegations moves forward.

The district attorney has said he plans to present the case to a grand jury. The independent reviews of Cornell’s handling of the matter are also ongoing.
