Cornell students hold public hearing, issue formal demands as controversy swells over alleged 2024 rape

BY Dana Damiani Ithaca
UPDATED 11:38 AM ET Oct. 02, 2026 PUBLISHED 8:02 AM ET Oct. 01, 2026 PUBLISHED 8:02 AM EDT Oct. 01, 2026
SHARE

Cornell University student leaders, alumni and community members are speaking out against what they’re calling a lack of transparency in the alleged 2024 rape case at a former fraternity house on campus. Formal demands were issued in a student public hearing on Thursday.

The hearing came amid renewed scrutiny of Cornell’s response to rape allegations made by an unnamed woman in a civil lawsuit. It alleges that she was sexually assaulted by seven men at the Chi Phi fraternity house in October 2024.

The law firm representing Jane Doe issued a statement, saying in part:

"The lawsuit specifically seeks to hold accountable those individuals and institutions who played a role in the commission of those same acts."

No criminal charges have been filed. The Tompkins County district attorney reopened the case following the lawsuit and public pressure. And now, Gov. Kathy Hochul has appointed Attorney General Letitia James to take over as special prosecutor.

Students told Spectrum News 1 on Thursday afternoon that they are not happy with how the administration handled the situation. 

“I am here to address the devastation that occurred in the school two years ago, and Cornell University’s continued failure to adequately address these acts of sexual violence," said one student during the hearing.

Many at the hearing shared their concerns publicly, searching for answers as to how this happened, why the situation was not addressed or better resolved two years ago and what improvements should be made to keep campus life safe.

While part of this meeting took place off the record to ensure student feedback was able to be comfortably provided, there was still a lot of discussion as to what should have been done and how the community can move forward.

The Cornell Student Assembly unanimously passed resolutions calling for administrators to publish a report on sexual assault incidents on campus, including disciplinary actions taken, and whether students found responsible would be allowed to graduate.

Students are also asking for transparency, calling for information on Cornell’s handling of the 2024 incident to be released. In addition, they are asking for the university, as well as campus police officials, to host a town hall to address students within 30 days.

In a statement on the university website, officials at the Ivy League school have confirmed that the fraternity house involved was barred from campus, and sanctions were issued, including suspension and expulsion, at the time of Jane Doe’s reporting two years ago. But they add that federal privacy laws prohibit Cornell from disclosing specific information regarding individual students and their disciplinary response.

Student leaders say what happened in 2024 was not an accurate representation of the Cornell community.

“It is an absolute shame of our civic justice system for there to be a court case that was only reopened because, according to the DA, the community asked for it," said Eeshaan Chaudhuri, president of the Cornell Student Assembly. "Every single person, all Jane and John Does, deserves their day in court. It is an absolute tragedy that it took a mass international media cycle to have this come up to the board. Something went wrong across the line and we want answers. What we're asking for is for the university to take accountability for exactly what transpired in 2024, and exactly what transpired in these past two weeks, and what led to two very different outcomes: one criminal case being closed, and one criminal case being reopened.”

As disturbing as the resurfacing of the allegations is from a former student, students are also reevaluating how safe they feel, all while trying to navigate their own thoughts about the institution compared to what they hear on TV and social media.

“The outrage from outside Cornell is kind of being misdirected a little bit. It is focusing more on the faculty and students rather than the institutions and policies that kind of failed Jane Doe and also the perpetrators themselves," said student Nicole Lin.

“The punishment was given by the university. So, the university also has that responsibility to administer proper punishment. And the fact that that wasn't done gives, it’s more on the university than on the student," said student Ethan Boniuk.

Some students said they are now seeing unfair discriminatory comments online against Cornell University students.

“A lot of the negativity and hatred is directed towards our male peers," said student Tiana Song.

There has been a constant media presence this week and students said they feel conflicted.

“We also are a little bit afraid of speaking to any of the journalists because we're also afraid of our words being misconstrued," Song said.

“I don't want our words to be kind of turned against us and represent the Cornell population in a negative way, because I do think the students here are great and most of the people here are good," said Lin.

Ithaca Mayor Robert Cantelmo is implementing a moratorium on Greek life, all student events and special event permits and other student group gatherings. The mayor says it will be in place until the university "conducts an accounting of its protocols to prevent and prosecute crimes of sexual violence and publicly releases new standards and practices to keep our community safe."
