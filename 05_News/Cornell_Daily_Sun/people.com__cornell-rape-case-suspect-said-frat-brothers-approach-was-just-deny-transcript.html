Man Accused in Cornell Rape Case Said Frat Brothers’ Approach Was ‘Just Deny’: Transcript

Members of the Chi Phi fraternity at Cornell University have been accused of drugging and gang raping an intoxicated woman in Oct. 2024

By
KC Baker
KC Baker

NEED TO KNOW

  • A former Cornell University undergraduate alleges in a civil complaint filed in September that she was drugged and raped by seven men starting on the night of Oct. 19, 2024, until about 5:45 a.m. the next morning
  • During a Title IX investigation by the university, one of the men allegedly said that the fraternity planned to deny any allegations about the incident, according to a record obtained by CBS News New York
  • The accused man also allegedly said he asked a sorority member to tell a friend to keep quiet about what she thought may have happened that night, the record says

One of the seven men accused of sexually assaulting a woman at Cornell University’s Chi Phi fraternity in 2024 allegedly told an investigator that fraternity members’ approach to rumors about the incident was to deny them, according to a Title IX record obtained by CBS News New York.

On the night of Oct. 19, 2024, a former Cornell University undergraduate alleges in a civil complaint filed in September against seven men that she was drugged and raped until about 5:45 a.m. the next morning.

In 2025, the university’s Office of Civil Rights and its Office of Student Conduct and Community Standards investigated the allegations “consistent with university policies and Title IX,” the university said in a statement online.

A Title IX investigation was conducted over several months and included hearings where a panel of trained faculty and staff heard evidence over multiple days, according to the university. 

The woman, identified as Jane Doe in the lawsuit, and the accused men had the opportunity to testify and present evidence in the university’s investigation to the hearing panel, which “issued a range of sanctions, which included expulsions and suspensions from Cornell” at its conclusion, the university said.

The Title IX record obtained by CBS News New York includes interviews with Doe, six of the seven men named in the lawsuit and 20 witnesses.

According to the record, a student who had been at the fraternity house on the night of the alleged assault said she received a call the next day from one of the men named in the lawsuit.

According to the record, the student, who was a member of a sorority, said the man called her to ask her to make sure that one of her friends didn’t “say anything that she thinks might’ve happened last night,” CBS News New York reports.

The man also told her that another fraternity member was “really concerned,” the student said, the record alleges.

The sorority sister said she gave her friend the message, but didn’t give the man’s request much more thought, the record alleges.

Weeks later, however, when the university issued a campus-wide alert about the incident, the sorority member’s friend told investigators that the man’s comments “clicked” and made “a lot more sense,” the record alleges, CBS News New York reports. “It was concerning.”

The friend and her roommate reported what the man said to campus police in a witness statement, the record states.

The witness statement, however, was not mentioned in the 565 pages of the Title IX record CBS News New York obtained, the outlet reports.

The record also includes an interview with the man, who initially denied asking the sorority member to tell her friend not to discuss what she thought may have happened that night.

A month later, the man changed what he said, the record alleges. In written corrections, he told the investigator that he “did talk to her at one point when she visited about not spreading any rumors,” the record alleges.

The man was also asked how the fraternity members dealt with rumors about what allegedly happened that night.

“We were just in the idea of just deny,” he allegedly replied.

Prosecutors did not pursue charges in 2024. 

Since the lawsuit was made public in a Sept. 18 story by The Cornell Daily Sun, Tompkins County District Attorney Matthew Van Houten said he would reopen the investigation.

Saying she lost faith in how the district attorney and campus police handled the investigation, New York Governor Kathy Hochul announced Thursday, Oct. 1, that she had signed an executive order appointing New York Attorney General Letitia James to oversee the case.

The Xi chapter of Chi Phi was closed in 2024 and remains barred from campus.

Three of the seven fraternity members have released statements through attorneys denying the allegations.

In a 2025 Cornell survey, 35% of undergraduate women said they had experienced nonconsensual sexual contact, up from 23% in 2023.

If you or someone you know has been sexually assaulted, please contact the National Sexual Assault Hotline at 1-800-656-HOPE (4673) or go to rainn.org.
